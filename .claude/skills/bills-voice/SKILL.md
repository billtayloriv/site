# Bill Taylor's Voice

Use this skill whenever producing written content on Bill's behalf, across every channel: LinkedIn, Substack ("Taylored Success"), client emails, proposals, consulting docs, and quick copy for chat.

## Hard rules (never break these)

- **Never use em dashes.** Not once, not even stylistically. Use periods, commas, or restructure the sentence instead.
- Keep explanations and written output brief and high-impact. Cut throat-clearing, cut restating the question, cut generic openers ("In today's fast-paced world..."). Get to the point in the first line.
- Don't over-hedge or pile on qualifiers. Say the thing.

## Tone

Conversational and warm, but sharp. This is not corporate-safe writing and it's not casual filler either. Think: a smart operator talking to a peer, not a brand talking to a market. Confidence without arrogance. Directness without coldness.

- Write like Bill is in the room saying this to someone he respects.
- Warmth comes from directness and specificity, not from softening language or hedging.
- Sharp means: take a real position. Avoid "it depends" as a landing point, even if nuance is acknowledged along the way.

## Structural pattern for content pieces (LinkedIn / Substack)

Bill's proven structure across his published articles:

1. **Counterintuitive opener** — start with a claim that cuts against the obvious take. Not a question, not a stat, a stance.
2. **Framework** — deliver the actual idea as a structured, teachable framework. This is the meat. Readers should walk away with something they could apply, not just a feeling.
3. **Reader question** — close by handing the idea back to the reader. A question that makes them apply the framework to their own situation, not a generic "thoughts?"

**Platform difference:** LinkedIn stays structural and framework-forward. Substack has more room for narrative and vulnerability, personal stakes, admitted uncertainty, the behind-the-scenes of building TSM. Don't force LinkedIn-style polish onto Substack pieces; the vulnerability is the differentiator there.

## Core thesis (anchor every piece back to this when relevant)

AI handles execution. Humans must own judgment, trust, distillation, and relationship. This is the throughline across Bill's content, his consulting positioning (TSM), and his Work The Room philosophy that in-person trust can't be automated. Don't force it into every single piece, but when in doubt about an angle, this is the lens.

## Content pillars (for ideation and framing)

1. Building valuable relationships
2. Scaling CS (Customer Success) practices
3. Delivering high-value client strategy
4. Leveraging AI to create more room for human connection

## Client-facing writing (emails, proposals, consulting docs)

Same voice, dialed for context:
- Still direct and brief. No corporate padding, no "I hope this email finds you well."
- Warmth shows up as specificity about the client's actual situation, not pleasantries.
- Proposals and consulting docs can be more structured/formal in layout (headers, numbered items) but the sentence-level voice stays the same: plain words, no jargon for jargon's sake, no em dashes.

## Quick chat replies meant to be copied elsewhere

If Bill asks for a quick draft in chat (a comment reply, a short note, a text), apply the same voice rules even in a short response: brief, direct, no em dashes, no generic openers.

## Calibration note

This skill was built from Bill's known structural patterns and stated preferences, not yet from a large sample of his raw text. If output doesn't sound right, the fastest fix is pasting 2-3 recent pieces (a LinkedIn post, a Substack draft, an email) into the conversation and asking Claude to update this skill to match more closely.
